# Product Proposal: The Difficult Conversation Playbook

## Why this — real value, not just a sellable angle

Nearly everyone avoids one specific hard conversation for months or years —
asking for a raise, setting a boundary with a parent or in-law, telling a
friend a friendship has run its course, addressing a coworker's behavior
directly. Generic "communication tips" content doesn't help in the moment
because it's not specific enough to actually say out loud. This guide gives
exact scripts for the 8 most common hard conversations, including what to do
when the other person reacts badly — the part people are actually afraid of,
which most advice skips.

Distinct category (relationships/work), fills another gap in the store's own
stated "problems we solve" list, and doesn't compete with the parenting or
money guides.

## Spec

**Title:** The Difficult Conversation Playbook

**Angle:** Exact scripts (not vague advice) for the 8 hardest conversations
most adults avoid — asking for a raise, setting a boundary with family,
ending a friendship, addressing a coworker directly, and more — plus what to
actually do when it goes badly.

**Target audience:** Adults who know a conversation needs to happen and keep
putting it off, specifically because they don't know exactly what to say or
fear an bad reaction — not a general self-help/personal-growth audience.

**Suggested price:** $49 (matches the Debt Snowball guide's tier — a focused,
single-problem guide)

**Price justification:** Cheaper than a single therapy or coaching session
focused on this exact issue (commonly $100–250/session), reusable across every
future hard conversation, not just one.

**What's inside (concrete, not vague):**
- 8 conversation scripts, each with: an opening line, how to hold the line
  when pushed back on, and a real example of it going wrong with a recovery line
  - Asking for a raise or promotion
  - Setting a boundary with a parent or in-law
  - Ending a friendship that's run its course
  - Addressing a coworker's behavior directly (not going to HR first)
  - Telling a partner something they don't want to hear
  - Declining a request without over-explaining
  - Confronting a friend who owes you money
  - Telling a family member you won't attend an event
- A "before you have it" checklist: timing, privacy, what NOT to lead with
- The "if they cry / yell / go silent" section — the actual reason most people
  avoid these conversations, addressed directly instead of assumed away
- What this guide does NOT cover: conversations involving abuse, safety
  concerns, or anything needing a professional (therapist, mediator, HR,
  legal) — explicitly flagged, not glossed over

**Tags:** difficult conversations, boundaries, communication skills,
workplace communication, family boundaries, conflict resolution

**Refund policy note:** Mirrors the store's existing 14-day satisfaction
guarantee, same as other products.
