# Product Proposal: The Debt Snowball Reality Check

## Why this — real value, not just a sellable angle

Most debt-payoff content is either generic ("just budget better!") or oversells a
single method as magic. The real gap: nobody shows the actual math of snowball
vs. avalanche side by side for a REAL set of numbers, or gives verbatim scripts
for the part people dread most — calling creditors. This guide does both,
honestly, including telling the reader which method costs them more in interest
(avalanche) vs. which one they're more likely to actually stick with
(snowball) — and lets them choose with real numbers instead of hype.

Distinct category from the existing catalog (parenting/sleep, side-hustle
income) — this is the "money" pillar the store's own About page already claims
("whatever problem you're facing — in parenting, money, health, relationships,
work") but has zero products for yet.

## Spec

**Title:** The Debt Snowball Reality Check

**Angle:** A working-through-debt guide that shows the real math (snowball vs.
avalanche, side by side, with actual numbers) instead of picking one method and
selling it as the only answer — plus the exact words to say when calling a
creditor, which is the step people avoid until it costs them more.

**Target audience:** Adults with $5,000–$50,000 in consumer debt (credit cards,
personal loans, medical debt) who feel overwhelmed by where to start — not
people in bankruptcy-level distress (this guide says explicitly when to see a
credit counselor or bankruptcy attorney instead).

**Suggested price:** $49 (lower than the $69–79 tier — this is a shorter,
single-problem guide, not a full multi-week program)

**Price justification:** Cheaper than one hour with a financial advisor
(commonly $150–300/hr) or a debt-settlement company's setup fee (often
$500–1,500) — reusable reference, no ongoing fee, no commission on the reader's
debt.

**What's inside (concrete, not vague):**
- Snowball vs. avalanche: a real worked example with 4 debts at different
  balances/rates, showing total interest paid and payoff time for both methods
- A "which one will you actually finish" honesty check (behavioral, not just
  math — acknowledges most people quit debt plans from demotivation, not bad math)
- Verbatim scripts: calling a creditor to ask for a lower rate, negotiating a
  settlement, disputing an error on a statement
- A one-page worksheet: list every debt, balance, rate, minimum payment — the
  actual first step most guides skip
- Clear, explicit "see a professional instead" flags: when debt exceeds ~50% of
  income, when collections/legal notices have started, when medical debt needs
  hospital financial-assistance programs instead of a payoff plan
- What NOT to do: debt-settlement companies' real fee structure and credit-score
  impact, explained plainly rather than assumed

**Tags:** debt payoff, debt snowball, debt avalanche, personal finance, credit
card debt, budgeting, money management

**Refund policy note:** Mirrors the store's existing 14-day satisfaction
guarantee (per the 2026-09-08 policy update) — same as other products, not a
separate policy.
